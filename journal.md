## Total Time Spent on the Project: **56 hours**

---

##  26/6 – 8 hours  
I began working on the project with a design of the basic idea: a four-chamber ventilation system that has been created to clean air on the basis of coffee grounds and some neutralizing substances like NH 3 and ZnCl 2. I was aiming to make a stream where each chamber was doing a specific job clean the air. I studied various processes of filtration which would be effective in the use of coffee grounds and could be combined effectively with the chemical neutralizers. At the end of the day, I already schemed a working flow diagram of the whole process and selected the Raspberry Pi 5 with a matching HAT to control various sensors and I/O control, the foundation of the system brain.

---

##  27/6 – 8 hours  
With the concept laid down, I started with CAD modeling where I used Fusion 360. I started forming the external enclosure, with the cutouts of chambers, and airflow directions. This involved space arrangements of fans and filters and chemical scrubber holders. I ensured that I left spaces to install sensors and internal conduction passages so as to leave room to make changes in future. Initial simulation of airflow demonstrated some bottle necks and hence I modified internal channel diameters to carry out maximum flow.

##  28/6 – 7 hours  
The whole day was spent perfecting CAD model more. Emphasis was on the incorporation of mount points of the Raspberry Pi and fans in a waterproof body. The system I designed can be user friendly, with holes designed in the LCD display and the external power switch. At the end of the day, I was able to create the first rendered image of the entire 3D unit. I created a simple, non-functional scale model by printing out a basic print-primarily so I can check the sizing and not so much the functionality.

![Rendered 3D prototype](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/eaa4ae09-3925-4c99-942b-1ccf35015177)

---

##  29/6 – 6 hours  
Instead, this turned toward the planning of the wiring layout in accordance with the sensor locations within the CAD model. I tested the positions of the servos virtually so that I could control air diversion between chambers. The way in which the Raspberry Pi simple inputs/outputs would engage with the selected servo valve was also an area that I investigated so that I could be sure that the system would be able to process control signals in a realistic manner without overloading itself.

---

I completed the CAD model ready to be documented and/or fabricated. I generated section diagrams and exploded views to make internal parts clear and their association. I added custom sensor mounting clips in the model too, and that they would be secure during use as well. After the structure was done, I printed the design files in form of STL and DXF to facilitate future manufacturing.

---

##  1/7 – 8 hours  
On this day, we were supposed to play around with the chemical processes of reaction taking place in the third chamber. I undertook computer simulation of various inner surface treatments to maximize reaction efficiency, dealing with the effect of ammonia-coffee grounds interaction in non-determinate airflow conditions. In the process, I was able to develop two equations to enable me to measure and make certain that the selected method of activation is efficient. I have also included brackets to hold coffee grounds and ZnCl 2 as well as filters in the chamber structure itself with hermetically sealed areas to trap any ammonia vapours. All these were noted down in my design notes to be used later.
